How they stack up

  2. GRID (68%, C+) is the best of the purpose-built infrastructure funds — decent breadth, calm ride.

  4. NLR, SRVR, AIRR bring up the rear (D grades, 40-48%) — NLR on weak momentum and high cost, SRVR on razor-thin liquidity (54K volume) despite the calmest ride of the nine, AIRR because it's simply a different theme.

Every fund in this group costs more (0.47-0.70% expense ratio) than the broad-market/semis ETFs graded above (0.09-0.35%) — thematic niche investing has a real, ongoing price tag baked in before a single dollar of return.

Grading Rubric

All 15 ETFs on this page are scored 1–5 on the same five dimensions, weighted, and summed to a percentage. Rev. 4: momentum's weight came back down to match the rest — every remaining dimension sits at an even 20%. Disagree with a weight? The raw 1–5 scores are shown on every card — re-weight by eye.

DimensionWeightWhat it measures
Momentum20% ↓ was 35%Current price's position within its 52-week range — closer to the high scores higher.
Diversification20%Holdings count & weighting method — how much rides on any single company.
Cost20% ↑ was 15%Expense ratio — lower is better, compounds over a 2–3yr hold
Liquidity20% ↑ was 15%Average daily volume — thin volume means wider spreads, worse fills
Risk, calmer20% ↑ was 15%52-week price swing — narrower range scores higher (easier to hold through)
AI purity0% — removedWas: how directly the fund is exposed to the AI-buildout thesis. Rewarding concentration in one theme was backwards at any weight, so it's gone rather than just discounted.
